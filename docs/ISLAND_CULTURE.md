## Learned rain superstitions

A completed player four-note demonstration or creature chime performance can open one 45-simulation-second prediction for each villager with actual nearby perception of the instrument. Music while already wet does not count. Rain crossing 0.35 within that window records a match; expiry without rain records a miss. A bounded 20-sample memory determines conviction, and failures lower it. Repeated notes never extend an open prediction.

With conviction at least 0.45, water below 45 and dry weather, an otherwise available villager may approach their remembered chime and play four alternating notes in hope of rain. The existing leisure motor and SharedRhythm visuals/audio handle it. An exclusive ritual lease protects against competing creature play, player notes and gift routines; grabbing the instrument cancels safely. A 180-second cooldown limits repetition. The ritual does not alter weather, water, energy or resources. Its actual completed music can itself be observed, so accidental future coincidence may reinforce the superstition.

Player throws, canceled/unconfirmed outcomes and duplicate Finish calls do not count. Late house/tree/villager harm retracts that throw's safe experiment and invalidates any matching unconsumed game lesson. Discovery, invitations, successful replays, preference and variety cooldown save in WorldPlayMemory; historical saves start without inferred experiments.

Near safe toy blocks, the creature can occasionally try a short roll before it knows the rule (20% style choice on an otherwise selected eligible ball activity). Safe repetition discovers the game. After discovery, remembered interest contributes to normal utility selection and preference affects the chance of choosing the style. It beckons/points briefly, then uses the existing reach/lift/roll/watch executor. A completed successful replay supports the existing one-shot A/B teaching window. Exploratory rolls use a 45-second cooldown; learned invitations use 90 seconds. Rebuilding/rearranging toy blocks provides another opportunity. This is bounded discovery within an authored interaction vocabulary, not a general rule-generation or language-model system.
